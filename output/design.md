# Campus Customs: Design Refresh (Problem 10)

**Goal:** make the site feel like a real, official Yale storefront. Modern and clean, full of school pride, and easy to shop, so visitors stay longer and buy.

## What we changed and why

| Area | What changed | Why it helps customers stay and buy |
|---|---|---|
| **Color** | Black and pink → **Yale Blue (#00356B) and white**, with Yale's secondary blues (#286DC0, #63AAFF) for accents and a soft blue-gray (#F5F8FC) for panels. Green and amber are used only for stock status. | Yale colors bring instant school pride and make the site look official. White space and a single strong brand color feel modern and trustworthy, so shoppers are more willing to buy. |
| **Fonts** | Same pairing as **yale.edu**: its CSS uses **Yale (YaleNew)**, a serif, for headings and **Mallory**, a humanist sans, for UI text. Both are licensed and can't go in a public repo, so we load close free matches, **EB Garamond** and **Source Sans 3**, with YaleNew and Mallory first in the stack for anyone who has them installed. | Serif headlines read as "Yale" and give an established, collegiate feel. The clean sans keeps prices, sizes and buttons easy to scan. |
| **Handsome Dan logo** | The **real Handsome Dan photo** (navy knit sweater, Y bow tie), shown as a circle cropped on his face with a white ring. It replaced an earlier CSS-drawn version at the user's request. It appears in the navbar logo, the hero (large, gently bobbing, ear wiggle), the chat button and avatar, the chat-results banner, the auth pages, the footer and the 404 page. | A friendly mascot gives the brand a face and something to recognize and remember. Putting Dan on the chat makes the assistant feel like part of the Yale family instead of a generic bot. |
| **Hierarchy** | A Yale Blue announcement bar ("Officially licensed Yale apparel · 57 Broadway"). A **full-width, solid Yale Blue hero band** (edge to edge and flush under the navbar, no gradient) with a big serif headline and two clear actions ("Shop the collection", "Help me find a gift"). A stats strip (102 styles · 14 residential colleges · 40+ years). Small uppercase "eyebrow" labels above every section title. A four-column footer. | Shoppers see what the store is, why to trust it, and where to click within seconds, which means fewer bounces. |
| **Product presentation** | Home now has **category tiles** with real product photos and live counts that link to the filtered Shop page. Cards show the garment type, a serif name, a bold price, **size chips** (sold-out sizes crossed out) and badges ("Few left" when 30 or fewer are in stock, "The Game" on Harvard–Yale gear), plus a "View details" hover pill. Product pages add a breadcrumb, hover-zoom on the photo, a trust row (Officially licensed · Live stock · 57 Broadway) and an **"Ask the CC assistant" look-alike button**. | Shoppers see sizes and scarcity without clicking, so they get to "I can buy this" faster. Badges create gentle urgency. Zoom and trust signals answer "is it real, is it good?" |
| **Motion** | Subtle only: cards lift and photos zoom on hover, sections fade up as you scroll, chat messages slide in, the chat panel pops open from the corner, and Dan bobs in the hero. Everything is turned off for users who set **prefers-reduced-motion**. | Movement makes the site feel alive and modern, and draws the eye to products and the chat, without getting in the way or hurting accessibility. |
| **Chat feel** | Blue header with Dan's avatar and "Ask CC · AI shop assistant", white bubbles with a Dan avatar for replies, Yale Blue bubbles for the shopper, "checking the shelves…" while it thinks, outlined blue suggestion chips, and a floating **"Ask CC"** pill with a green online dot. | It looks like a messaging app people already use, which invites them to start a conversation. And conversations lead to product cards and sales. |
| **Chat voice** | The agent now talks like a **friendly Yale student who works at the shop**: casual, warm, the occasional emoji or campus reference (Bass, Old Campus, the Game, cold New Haven winters), light Harvard ribbing. It adapts to formal shoppers such as parents. It says plainly that it's an **AI assistant** if asked whether it's a real student, and never makes up a name or class year. Prices and stock stay exact. | A peer voice feels like getting a recommendation from a friend, not a sales script, so shoppers keep chatting. Being honest about being an AI keeps their trust. |
| **Fills the screen** | The layout is fluid: the root font size scales with the window (`clamp(15px, 1.05vw, 44px)`), and the container is rem-based, so text, cards and the hero grow on big monitors instead of sitting in a narrow column. | On a large or zoomed-out screen the store fills the view like a real retail site. It no longer looks tiny and empty, and products are easier to see. |
| **Extras we added** | Mobile layout: a shorter announcement bar, "Sign up" instead of "Create account", and a round chat button. Auth pages split into a Yale Blue welcome panel and the form. The page scrolls to the top on navigation. The favicon is a Yale Blue "CC". There's a page `<meta description>` and `theme-color`. Keyboard focus rings use Yale's light blue. | The site works well on phones, where most students shop. Small polish details are what make a site feel "real". |

## Verified in the running app

- Home, Shop, product, login, and the chat panel were all checked in the browser at desktop width and at mobile width (375 px). There's no horizontal scroll on mobile.
- Category tiles link to `/products?category=…` with matching counts (Hoodies 27 · Crewnecks 30 · T-shirts 26 · Quarter-zips 11 · Jackets 8). These are the same numbers as the Shop filters, because both pages use one shared `categories.ts`.
- Chat voice, live:
  - "what should I wear to the harvard game? its gonna be freezing" → layered picks and "Harvard can wait, but your fingers can't."
  - "are you a real yale student?" → "Nope, I'm Campus Customs' AI shopping assistant…"
  - A formal parent message got a polite, formal reply.
- "Ask the CC assistant" on a product page opened the chat and returned similar in-stock hoodies as cards. Asking for crewnecks still fills the page (30 results, under the new Dan banner).
